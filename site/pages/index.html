What happens after a new drug is approved in the US and Europe?

An approval date is not the same as patient access. This atlas follows all {{n}} new medicines approved by both the FDA and the European Commission in 2019–2025. It tracks each one from first approval to benefit assessment in Germany, France and England and to reimbursement in Italy. Every number links to its public source.

Three different things. The atlas keeps apart the value recognised by an assessment body (G-BA in Germany, HAS in France, NICE in England), reimbursement (in Italy, publication in the Gazzetta Ufficiale; in Germany, from launch) and actual availability to patients (regional formularies, early-access routes such as Italy's Law 648/96 and the AIFA 5% fund). It measures the first two. Actual availability, the closest to what patients experience, is not measured.

CountryMilestone measuredHow it relates to funding
GermanyG-BA decision (about six months after launch)The drug is reimbursed from launch; the assessment informs the negotiated price.
FranceHAS Transparency Committee opinionThe assessment comes before funding.

The four scales answer different questions with different criteria and comparators, so they are shown side by side, not scored against each other. AIFA innovativeness is decided only together with reimbursement, so it covers the {{it_innov_n}} reimbursed drugs whose determination could be read. Full innovativeness gives access to the innovative-medicines fund and, by law, to regional formularies without waiting for regional inclusion; how regions apply this is not measured.
